Growth Deceleration

Margin debt is still growing year-over-year, but the rate of growth is slowing down. The second derivative (rate of change of the growth rate) is -7.8, indicating negative acceleration.

This deceleration pattern historically appears before major market peaks, as seen in 2000, 2007, and 2021.

Elevated Growth Levels

Current year-over-year growth of 36.3% is in the 90th percentile of all readings since 1997. This means only 10% of historical periods showed higher growth rates.

From this percentile level, margin debt growth typically mean-reverts to 10-15% within six months as investors reduce leverage.

What is margin debt?

Money investors borrow from brokers to buy stocks. Rising = confidence/risk-taking. Falling = deleveraging. Peaks often precede market tops (2000, 2007, 2021).

What the Thresholds Mean

Late-Cycle (30-60%)

Investors are highly leveraged. Growth is elevated but unsustainable. Historically precedes market peaks. Expect either gradual cooling to 10-15% or spike to 60%+ before crash.

Extreme (60%+)

Bubble conditions. Investors borrowing at unsustainable rates. Sharp reversals follow. Examples: March 2000 (dot-com), July 2007 (crisis), Feb 2021 (meme stocks).
